Interpreting your results

Like all statistical estimates, these results rest on assumptions. The method assumes your lists all draw from the same population, that the population is reasonably stable over the period the lists cover, and that the model adequately captures how the lists relate to one another. Different but equally plausible assumptions can lead to different estimates. These results are best treated as an evidence-based indication of scale rather than a precise count. If the estimate will inform high-stakes decisions, such as policy, funding, or legal proceedings, we recommend reviewing the analysis with a statistician.
