Multiple Systems Estimation

About this tool

When working on issues like modern slavery, drug use, homelessness, or human rights abuses, the people most affected are often hidden. No single organisation sees everyone. The police know about some people, hospitals know about others, charities and local authorities know about others still, and many people don't appear in anyone's records at all.

This calculator estimates how many people are missing from all of those records. It uses a well-established technique called Multiple Systems Estimation, which has been used to estimate the number of victims of modern slavery in the UK and to count hidden populations in conflict zones around the world.

How it works

The method relies on a simple idea. If you have several lists of people drawn from the same population, some people will appear on more than one list. The amount of overlap between the lists tells us something about how visible this population is. Lots of overlap suggests most people are being seen by at least one organisation. Very little overlap suggests there are many people that no one is seeing. The calculator uses these overlap patterns to estimate the number of people who appear on no list at all, sometimes called the "dark figure". Adding this to the number of people you have observed gives an estimate of the total population size.

Behind the scenes, the calculator uses a modern statistical method developed at the University of Birmingham. The hard computational work has already been done in advance, so you get results in seconds rather than hours, and you don't need any specialist software, powerful computers, or statistical training. The method also works when some of your counts have been suppressed or redacted to protect people's identities, which is common when working with sensitive administrative data.

What you need

For each list and each combination of lists, you need to know how many people appeared there. For example, how many people were known only to organisation 1 , how many were known to both organisation 1 and organisation 2, and so on. You never need to upload any information that could identify an individual. You can either enter these counts directly on the next page or upload them as a CSV file.

What you get

The calculator gives you an estimate of the number of unobserved people, together with a range reflecting how certain the estimate is. The true number is unlikely to be a single exact figure, so this range is an important part of the result and we recommend reporting it alongside the estimate. You can download your results, along with the data you entered, for your records.
